Step 1: Physical Sensor Wiring & 5V Safety

The YF-S201 Hall sensor requires 5V VCC to spin reliably. Its yellow signal wire outputs a 5V square wave. Because ESP32 GPIO pins are not 5V tolerant (3.3V max):

  • Use a 1kΩ + 2kΩ resistor divider to step 5V pulses down to 3.3V.
  • Tie sensor 5V ground and ESP32 GND together (mandatory common ground).
  • Connect Signal 1 to GPIO 27 and Signal 2 to GPIO 26.
Step 2: Volumetric Bucket Calibration

Never assume a generic calibration factor. Individual turbine impellers vary:

  • Dispense exactly 5.0 Litres of water into a calibrated bucket.
  • Count raw interrupt pulses in ESP32 serial monitor (e.g. 2,250 pulses).
  • Calculate factor: K = 2250 / 5.0 = 450.0 pulses/Litre.
  • Store separate calibration constants for Sensor 1 and Sensor 2.
Step 3: Seamless Software Bridge Replacement

The AquaGuard backend is architected around the WaterDataService interface:

  • Create FirebaseWaterDataService or an MQTT/REST subscriber class.
  • Annotate it with @Primary @Service.
  • Spring Boot automatically injects the live hardware telemetry into all 12 controllers and views with zero UI refactoring!
Developer & Presentation Troubleshooting Guide

Run netstat -ano | findstr :8080 in Windows PowerShell to find the PID. Terminate it with taskkill /F /PID <PID>, or change server.port=8081 in src/main/resources/application.properties.

Leakage evaluation uses sequential cumulative-volume readings from Firebase. It reports insufficient data until matched inlet and outlet samples are available; it does not fabricate a leak event.

Chart.js is loaded from the public CDN (jsdelivr). If presenting without active internet connectivity on your laptop, copy chart.umd.min.js into src/main/resources/static/js/ and link locally.
